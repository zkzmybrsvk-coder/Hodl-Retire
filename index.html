On default settings, borrowing against your stack to retire looks great on a smooth curve but survives only ~9% of volatile futures. Pairing it with a cash reserve that lets you skip borrowing in down years takes survival to ~76%. That reserve — not the power law, not the reclaim mechanic — is the load-bearing element of this whole strategy.

▸ Run Them In Order

1 → 2: Design a plan that looks good, then stress it. If Monte Carlo survival is under ~70%, the planner was lying to you — turn on the pause defense.

3: Sanity-check whether borrowing beats simply selling. Sometimes the tax deferral is the only real edge.

4: The synthesis — find the borrow→sell switch year that holds up under volatility. Notice the pause defense matters more than the switch timing.

5: Size the cash reserve the pause defense actually requires. This is often the sobering number — it can be very large.

6: See which assumption your plan is most fragile to, so you stress-test what matters (usually stack size and budget — rarely the loan rate).

These are distinct questions asked at different stages, not interchangeable views. Opening the stress tests deliberately is the friction you want — the optimistic planner shouldn't be trusted on its own.

⚠ Educational Only — Not Advice Every tool here rests on assumptions: the power-law model, a simplified volatility process with no fat tails or regime changes, flat-rate tax, and no counterparty-failure risk (a lender insolvency is a real total-loss scenario none of these model). The pause defense assumes you actually hold enough outside cash for multiple consecutive down years. Treat all four as stress tests, never promises. Not financial or tax advice.
